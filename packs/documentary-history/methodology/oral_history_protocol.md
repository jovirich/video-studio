# Oral history and traditional knowledge protocol

Governs T4 sources. Oral tradition frequently preserves what no archive recorded;
it also comes with obligations that written sources do not.

## 1. Before approaching anyone

- [ ] Cultural Advisor consulted; the approach route agreed
- [ ] The recognised channel identified — an unannounced approach to a knowledge
      holder can itself be a breach
- [ ] Fee agreed and budgeted. Knowledge holders are contributors, not subjects.
- [ ] Interpreter engaged where needed, and briefed on the subject matter
- [ ] Consent form translated into the language of the conversation
- [ ] It is understood which knowledge is **restricted** and must not be asked for

That last point is the one that goes wrong. Some knowledge is restricted by
initiation, office, gender, or age. Asking for it puts the holder in an impossible
position — refusing a guest is costly, and disclosing is a breach. **Establish the
boundary with the advisor before the conversation, not during it.**

## 2. Consent

Template: [../../templates/legal/interview_consent.md](../../templates/legal/interview_consent.md).

Must state, in the holder's language:

- who the studio is and what the work is,
- where it will be shown, in what media, for how long,
- that the recording will be retained and archived, and where,
- **whether any AI processing will be applied** — transcription, translation, voice
  processing — stated explicitly. Consent that does not cover this is not valid for
  this studio's purposes.
- that the recording will **not** be used to train or synthesise a voice,
- what they may withhold, and that withholding costs them nothing,
- their right of review before broadcast,
- their right to withdraw, and honestly what withdrawal can and cannot undo after
  release,
- attribution: named, anonymised, or withheld — their choice, revisitable.

## 3. Recording

- Spec per [../../standards/delivery_specs.md](../../standards/delivery_specs.md)
  § Interview capture. Two recorders, always.
- Record the **context of transmission**: who taught the holder this, in what
  setting, on what occasions it is normally recounted. This is as much a part of the
  evidence as the content, and it is what a critique block needs.
- Record the holder's **standing**: lineage, office, training, the basis on which
  they hold this knowledge. "An elder from the village" is not standing.
- Do not interrupt a formal recounting. Ask afterwards.
- Room tone. Always.

## 4. Transcription and translation

- Transcribe in the original language first, then translate. Never transcribe
  straight into English — the intermediate step is where meaning is lost silently.
- The transcriber and translator are named, and are credited.
- Machine transcription and translation are permitted as a **first pass only**, are
  fully human-reviewed, and are only used where the consent covers AI processing.
- Untranslatable terms are kept and glossed rather than approximated.
- The holder reviews the transcript where practical.

## 5. Evidentiary handling

Per [../../bible/02_evidence_and_sourcing.md](../../bible/02_evidence_and_sourcing.md) §2.1:

- T4 material carries the **`traditional`** confidence register with named
  attribution: "In the accounts kept by X…", never "It is said that…".
- **Volume does not promote a tradition to `established`.** Ten holders of the same
  tradition are one tradition, not ten corroborating sources.
- A T4 account may **contradict** a T1 colonial record on screen, with both stated.
  This is often the most valuable thing an episode can do, and the tier numbers must
  not be read as ranking whose knowledge counts.
- Where a tradition and material evidence converge, that convergence is itself a
  finding worth stating.
- Variation between holders is data, not error. Record it rather than reconciling it.

## 6. After

- [ ] Fee paid, promptly. Not on the studio's payment terms — on the holder's.
- [ ] Copy of the recording provided to the holder, in an accessible format
- [ ] Copy deposited with the community's chosen institution where they wish it
- [ ] Credit as agreed
- [ ] Finished episode shown to the holder before or at release
- [ ] Source record completed with the `oral_protocol` block

## 7. Restricted material

If restricted knowledge is disclosed — deliberately or otherwise:

1. Stop. Do not transcribe it further.
2. Flag the recording as restricted; it does not enter the general asset store.
3. Raise it with the Cultural Advisor.
4. The default is that it is **not used**, and the recording is returned or
   destroyed at the holder's direction.

A recording that exists is a recording that can leak. The only reliable protection
is not keeping it.

## 8. Community-held knowledge

Some knowledge belongs to a community rather than an individual. An individual's
consent may not be sufficient to license it. Where this is the case, the advisory
register records the community's decision-making body and the agreement reached.
The studio does not decide for itself that an individual's consent was enough.
