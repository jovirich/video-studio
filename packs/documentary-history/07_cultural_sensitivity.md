# 07 — Cultural sensitivity and representation

## 1. Why this has teeth rather than good intentions

A studio depicting histories that belong to living communities, using tools that can
manufacture any image on request, has an unusual capacity to do harm quickly and at
scale. Good intentions do not scale; process does. This document gives one role —
the Cultural Advisor — the authority to stop work, and defines when it is used.

## 2. Categories requiring advisory review before generation

Not before publication. **Before generation.** Once a striking image exists, the
conversation about whether it should exist becomes much harder.

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Sacred and ritual | Shrines, deities, initiatory practice, ritual objects, divination, ceremonies with restricted attendance |
| Masquerade and regalia | Masks, masquerade performance, royal and priestly regalia, insignia of office |
| Human remains and burial | Burial sites, funerary practice, remains in collections, ancestor veneration |
| Living lineages | Depiction of named ancestors of living families; succession and title disputes |
| Atrocity and enslavement | Depictions of violence, captivity, trafficking, and their victims |
| Contested territory and title | Land, chieftaincy, borders, and origin claims that bear on current disputes |
| Bodies | Nudity, scarification, body modification, and how they are framed |
| Language and naming | Which name form a people or place is given; ethnonyms with contested or imposed origins |

## 3. Standing prohibitions

- No generation or depiction of material whose viewing is restricted by initiation,
  gender, age, or office within the tradition concerned — including material that
  circulates freely online. Wide availability is not consent.
- No depiction of human remains from a specific identifiable community without that
  community's agreement.
- No use of sacred music or speech as underscore.
- No compositing of a real sacred object into a generated scene.
- No depiction of a named living person without consent.

## 4. Advisory hold

Any contributor may file a `[sensitivity]` issue, which places an **advisory hold**
on the affected asset, shot, sequence, or record. Under hold:

- No further generation on the held item.
- The item cannot enter an edit or pass any gate.
- The hold is released only by a written ruling from the Cultural Advisor, recorded
  in the line's advisory register.

**The Showrunner cannot unilaterally release a hold.** Disagreement escalates to a
documented ruling with the advisory contact for the relevant community. This is the
one structural check on the Showrunner's editorial authority, and it exists because
the cost of being wrong here is borne by people outside the studio.

## 5. Advisory board

Each production line maintains an advisory register naming:
- the advisors, their standing and expertise, and what they are and are not
  competent to rule on,
- the communities and traditions covered, and the gaps not yet covered,
- terms: fee, credit, review rights, and the right to withdraw.

Advisors are paid. An advisor who is not paid is not an advisor; they are a favour
being taken advantage of, and they will eventually and rightly stop answering.

**A line does not begin production on material outside its advisory coverage.** If
the register has no one competent on a tradition an episode needs, the episode waits.

## 6. Representation standards

- **Specificity over generality.** "African" is not a culture. Nor is "Nigerian".
  Every depiction names the people, place, and period it depicts, and the shot
  record carries them. Generative models default hard to pan-African pastiche and
  will produce it unless the prompt and the review actively prevent it.
- **Agency.** Historical subjects act; they are not acted upon by history. Passive
  framing of African societies as objects of external forces is a recurring failure
  of the genre and is checked for at script lock.
- **Internal complexity.** Societies contain conflict, class, dissent, and change.
  Depicting a people as uniform is a form of flattening even when the tone is
  admiring.
- **Colonial framing.** Colonial-era categories — "tribe", "chief", "primitive",
  "discovery", "pre-history" for periods with rich records — are interrogated, not
  inherited. See [../standards/prohibited_language.md](../standards/prohibited_language.md).
- **The corrective trap.** Overclaiming grandeur to counter denigration is still
  overclaiming, and it hands critics an easy target. The defence against a
  distorted record is an accurate one.

## 7. Working with communities

- Approach through recognised channels; the line's advisory register records who
  they are.
- State plainly what the material will be used for, where it will be shown, and for
  how long. Consent for a documentary is not consent for a trailer, a thumbnail, or
  a training set.
- Provide the finished work to the community before or at release, in a form they
  can actually access.
- Where a community asks that something not be shown, the default is to comply. If
  the studio believes there is an overriding public-interest reason, it is argued in
  writing, ruled on by the Cultural Advisor, and disclosed on screen.

## 8. Consent

Templates in [../templates/legal/](../templates/legal/). Every consent record states:
scope of use, media, territories, duration, whether AI processing is permitted on
the material, right of review, and right of withdrawal (and what withdrawal can and
cannot undo after release). Consent obtained without stating the AI processing scope
is not valid for this studio's purposes.

## 9. Review

The sensitivity gate runs at three points: at greenlight (does the premise clear?),
before generation (do these prompts clear?), and at picture lock (does the cut
clear?). Checklist: [../ops/checklists/sensitivity_review.md](../ops/checklists/sensitivity_review.md).
