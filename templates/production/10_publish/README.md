# 10_publish

The text that travels further than the film. Descriptions get quoted, credits get
checked by the people named in them, and the AI-use statement is the one thing a
sceptical viewer will look for before deciding whether to trust anything else.

## What goes here

| File | Template | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `description.md` | [_TEMPLATE_description.md](_TEMPLATE_description.md) | Platform description, chapters, tags, and the sources link |
| `credits.md` | [_TEMPLATE_credits.md](_TEMPLATE_credits.md) | Every contributor, named, in the form they chose |
| `credits_ai_statement.md` | [credits_ai_statement.md](credits_ai_statement.md) | The reusable AI-use block, filled per production |
| `cue_sheet.csv` | [_TEMPLATE_cue_sheet.csv](_TEMPLATE_cue_sheet.csv) | Every music cue, its usage, and its rights basis |
| `provenance_summary.md` | generated | Per-shot provenance, from the frozen manifest |
| `sources.md` | generated | The bibliography, from the claim and source registry |

`provenance_summary.md` and `sources.md` are generated by `studio_ops report`
(**NOT BUILT** — [../../../docs/status.md](../../../docs/status.md)). They are not
hand-written: a hand-written provenance summary is an assertion about the manifest
rather than a view of it, and the two diverge on the first re-render.

## The cue sheet

One row per cue, including generated music. Columns cover identity, placement,
`usage_type` (background, featured, theme, logo), origin, the rights chain, and the
territories and term actually granted. Generated cues additionally carry the tool
and the prompt card that produced them, because "who wrote this" has an answer for
them too and it is not a person.

`cleared` is a boolean, and a `false` in that column blocks the rights gate. The
sheet ships with one placeholder row using obviously-fake IDs — delete it.

An incomplete cue sheet is discovered at the rights gate, one stage after it was
created, which is why it is built during [../07_audio_post/](../07_audio_post/) and
merely finished here. A cue sheet assembled after delivery is assembled from memory.

## Before this stage starts

- **Technical QC is signed.** Publishing precedes nothing; it follows everything.

## Publication is an act, not a gate

There is no publish gate, and the absence is deliberate. A gate certifies a claim
against a checklist; publication asserts nothing new. Everything that could be
certified was certified upstream, and adding a ceremonial gate here would create the
impression that publication is the moment of accountability. It is not — it is the
moment accountability becomes public.

What follows publication is the corrections process, which is the part of the
system most likely to be neglected and most visible when it is.
[../../records/_TEMPLATE_correction.md](../../records/_TEMPLATE_correction.md).

## Fact-check applies here too

The description is checked to the same standard as narration. It is written last,
quickly, usually by whoever is available, and it is the text most likely to be
quoted back at the studio out of context. Every factual statement in it carries a
claim reference in the working copy, stripped at publication like any other.

Titles and thumbnail text are held to the same standard. A thumbnail that overstates
what the episode establishes is the same failure as a line of narration that does,
with wider reach and no accompanying qualification.

## Disclosure at publication

Four levels, all required
([../../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md](../../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md) §3):

1. **In-frame mark** on every reconstruction and interpretive shot — done at edit.
2. **An explainer card** at the first such shot — done at edit.
3. **A credits statement** naming every generative tool used, by category —
   [credits_ai_statement.md](credits_ai_statement.md).
4. **A public methodology page**, plus a per-production provenance summary published
   alongside the film.

Level 4 is unusual, and it is the point. A viewer who wants to know exactly which
shots were generated, with which tool, from which prompt, can find out. That is a
far stronger claim on trust than a blanket "some imagery is AI-assisted", which
tells a viewer nothing they can check.
