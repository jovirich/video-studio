# Legal templates

> ## These are starting points. They are not legal advice.
>
> **Every document in this directory must be reviewed by a qualified lawyer
> admitted in the relevant jurisdiction before it is put in front of anybody.**
>
> They are drafted by people who make films, not by people who practise law. They
> have not been reviewed by counsel in any jurisdiction. They do not account for
> the law where you are, where the other party is, or where the work will be
> distributed — and those are frequently three different places with three
> different answers.
>
> Using one of these unreviewed is not a shortcut. A consent that does not hold is
> worse than no consent: it produces a false record of permission, and the person
> who relied on it finds out at the point where the material is already published.

Nothing here creates a lawyer-client relationship, and nothing here is a
representation that any of it is enforceable anywhere.

## What a lawyer must look at, at minimum

Take this list to them rather than asking whether the document is "OK":

1. **Jurisdiction and governing law.** Which law applies, where disputes are heard,
   and whether that choice is even effective given where the other party lives.
2. **Data protection.** Recordings of identifiable people are personal data almost
   everywhere. Consent is often not the correct lawful basis, and where it is, it
   must be specific, informed, and withdrawable — which constrains how these
   documents can be written.
3. **Moral rights**, which in many jurisdictions cannot be waived at all, and in
   others can only be waived with specific wording.
4. **Whether the AI processing terms mean what they say** in the relevant law, and
   whether a no-training term binds a downstream vendor the studio does not
   contract with.
5. **Minors, and adults who may lack capacity.** None of these templates addresses
   either, and both need a different instrument.
6. **Collective and community rights.** An individual's consent does not carry a
   community's, and in some jurisdictions and many customary systems, knowledge is
   not an individual's to give.
7. **Whether translation is required** for the document to be validly agreed. A
   consent form the signatory cannot read is not informed consent, whatever it says
   about itself.

## What these templates are designed to do

Even before review, they are shaped by three commitments that come from the
platform's canon rather than from law:

**Consent is specific, or it is not consent.** A blanket permission covering "any
use, in any media, in perpetuity" is easy to obtain, easy to sign, and tells the
signatory nothing about what will actually happen. Every template here enumerates
the use, the territory, the term, and the media.

**AI processing is named, not buried.** Anyone recorded is told plainly what will be
done with the recording by machine — transcription, translation, cleanup — and told
plainly what will not: **it will not be used to train a model, and it will not be
used to synthesise a voice.** That commitment is
[../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md](../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md) §2,
and it is not negotiable per production.

**Withdrawal is honest about its limits.** A right to withdraw that is described as
absolute is a promise the studio cannot keep once a film is public and copied. Every
template here says precisely what withdrawal removes, what it cannot remove, and by
when it must be exercised for each. Being honest about this in advance is harder and
is the only version that survives being tested.

## Working practice

- The instrument is agreed **before** the recording, not after. Consent obtained
  after the fact is consent obtained from someone who already knows how much work
  they would be undoing.
- The signed instrument gets a clearance record (`CLR-*`) and is referenced from
  every record that relies on it — source records, character records, prompt cards,
  manifest entries.
- Where the contributor's language differs from the document's, a translation is
  provided and the translator is named.
- A copy goes to the contributor. Always. A one-sided agreement that only one side
  holds is not a record; it is an assertion.
