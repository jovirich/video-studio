# Translator agreement

> **STARTING POINT ONLY — NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Must be reviewed by a qualified
> lawyer admitted in the relevant jurisdiction before use, including on
> copyright in translations, which subsists separately from the source text in
> most jurisdictions. See [README.md](README.md).

## 2. Machine translation

Where the studio provides a machine-generated draft, the translator is engaged to
**review and correct** it, not to certify it.

| | |
|---|---|
| Fee basis for reviewing a draft | TBD — reviewing a bad machine draft frequently takes longer than translating from scratch, and the rate should reflect that rather than assume a discount |
| Right to reject the draft | The translator may discard a machine draft entirely and translate afresh, at the agreed rate for translation |
| Attribution | The finished translation is the translator's work and is credited to them, regardless of whether a draft was used |

TBD — name the machine translation tools used, and confirm from the model terms
register that their terms permit the use and do not train on inputs. Unpublished or
restricted source material does not go to a third-party endpoint at all.

## 3. Credit

| | |
|---|---|
| Credit form | TBD — named / anonymised / withheld, at the translator's choice |
| Exact wording | TBD — as the translator writes it, with correct diacritics |
| Placement | TBD — credits, and beside on-screen quotations where practicable |

**Every translation used on screen is credited**, including translations of short
quotations. An uncredited translation presents an editorial act as a transcription —
the viewer reads the English as what the source says, rather than as one person's
rendering of it.

## 5. Editorial control over the translation

| | |
|---|---|
| Changes to the translation | TBD — the studio does not alter a credited translation without the translator's agreement |
| Trimming for caption length | TBD — where caption limits force a shortening, the translator makes the shortening or approves it |
| Disagreement | TBD — where the studio and the translator disagree on a rendering, what happens: normally the translator's version stands or the credit is removed |

A translation shortened to fit a caption box by someone who does not speak the
language is a new translation with the original translator's name on it.

## 6. Orthography

TBD — which standard applies, who decided, and what the translator does when a
source uses a different one.

Orthography is an editorial decision, not a technical one: several standards usually
exist, and choosing between them is a position on whose written form is authoritative.
The line's decision is recorded in its `languages/` folder and the translator works
to it, raising disagreement rather than silently normalising.
