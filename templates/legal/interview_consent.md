## 3. AI and machine processing — what will be done

The studio will use automated tools on this recording for:

- [ ] **Transcription** — machine-generated first pass, corrected by a named person
- [ ] **Translation** — machine-generated draft, reviewed and corrected by a named
      translator who is credited
- [ ] **Audio cleanup** — noise reduction and levelling that repairs the recording
      and does **not** alter what was said
- [ ] **Search and indexing** within the studio's own research materials
- [ ] TBD — anything else, named specifically

Where any of these uses a third-party service, the recording leaves the studio's
systems. TBD — name the services, or state that only local/self-hosted tools are
used. If the material is restricted, sensitive, or unpublished, the studio's own
canon requires local tools unless the vendor contract carries a no-training term and
this consent covers the use.

## 4. AI and machine processing — what will NOT be done

The studio commits that this recording **will not**:

1. **Be used to train, fine-tune, or adapt any machine-learning model**, by the
   studio or by any party the studio provides it to.
2. **Be used to synthesise, clone, or reproduce the contributor's voice**, in any
   form, for any purpose, including a "reference read" that is later discarded.
3. **Be used to generate a synthetic likeness** of the contributor.
4. **Be altered by any tool in a way that changes the meaning of what was said** —
   including re-timing, re-voicing, or removing hesitation in a way that changes how
   a statement reads.

These are not negotiable per production. They are platform canon
([../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md](../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md) §2)
and they bind every production this material could be used in.

Any use of the contributor's voice or likeness beyond the above requires a separate
instrument: [likeness_and_voice_consent.md](likeness_and_voice_consent.md).

| What a review can change | TBD — factual corrections to their own words, always; withdrawal of a passage; anonymisation |
| What it cannot change | TBD — the production's editorial conclusions, which remain the studio's |

*Why the last two rows are separate:* a review right that is described as absolute is
either untrue or hands editorial control to the contributor. Saying which parts are
theirs and which are the studio's, in advance, is what makes the right usable rather
than a source of a later argument.

## 7. Right of withdrawal — and what it can and cannot undo

The contributor may withdraw consent at any time, in writing, to TBD — contact.

**Being honest about this is more important than sounding generous.**

| When withdrawal happens | What the studio will do | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **Before publication** | Remove the contribution entirely from the production and delete or restrict the recording as the contributor directs | — |
| **After publication, before a re-cut** | Remove the contribution at the next version; annotate the published version; stop using it in any new material, clip, or trailer | Recall copies already downloaded, mirrored, re-uploaded, or archived by third parties |
| **After publication, long term** | Remove from any platform under the studio's control; refuse all future use; delete the source recording on request | Undo the fact of publication. A film that has been public has been seen, quoted, and copied, and no clause changes that. |

TBD — state the deadline, if any, after which removal from a published work is no
longer offered, and be explicit that this is a practical limit rather than a
judgement about the request.

The studio will not require a reason for withdrawal and will not ask the contributor
to justify it.
