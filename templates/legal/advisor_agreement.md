| Materials reviewed | TBD — briefs, outlines, scripts, prompt sets, cuts |

*Why "outside scope" is written down:* an advisor engaged on one subject is routinely
asked about an adjacent one under deadline pressure, and an answer given informally
is later cited as advice. Bounding the engagement protects the advisor from being
credited with a position they never took.

**The fee is agreed before the work.** The studio's own canon makes
`consultation_fees_budgeted: false` a red flag at greenlight, precisely because
settling this later means asking someone to work unpaid after the budget is spent.
([../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md](../../core/01_provenance_and_ai_disclosure.md) §7.)

Where the advisor is speaking for or on behalf of a community rather than from
individual expertise, TBD — state what is being paid, to whom, and how that was
agreed with the community. An individual fee is not a community's agreement.

## 4. Review rights

| | |
|---|---|
| The advisor reviews | TBD — brief, outline, script, prompt set, locked cut |
| Provided by | TBD — a stated period before each gate, long enough to be real |
| Response period | TBD |
| Form of response | TBD — written, so it can be recorded as a ruling |

The advisor is **given the opportunity to review the material they informed before
it airs**. That is a platform commitment, not a courtesy, and it means providing the
material early enough that a substantive response is possible. A cut sent forty-eight
hours before delivery has been shown, not reviewed.

## 5. Authority

| | |
|---|---|
| Advisory hold | The advisor may place a hold on any item. The hold takes effect immediately and is released only by the advisor, in writing. |
| Override | TBD — normally: the hold is the one authority the Showrunner cannot unilaterally override |
| Rulings | Recorded as `ADV-*` records, with reasoning, and referenced by every record they govern |
| Editorial authority | TBD — final editorial authority remains with the studio, and the advisor's role is advisory except in respect of the hold |

*Why the last row is stated:* an advisor who believes they have final cut and a
studio that believes otherwise will find out during a disagreement. Both positions
are workable; the mismatch is not.

## 6. Right to withdraw

The advisor may withdraw at any time, in writing, without giving a reason.

| | What happens |
|---|---|
| Credit | TBD — removed at the advisor's option, at the next version. The advisor decides whether their name stays. |
| Work already done | TBD — whether the studio may continue to rely on advice already given, and whether the advisor may require that it be disregarded |
| Fee | TBD — what is payable for work done to that point. Settle this now; settling it during a withdrawal makes the withdrawal a negotiation. |
| Published work | TBD — what withdrawal removes from a published production, and honestly what it cannot: copies already distributed cannot be recalled |
| Public statement | TBD — whether either party may state that the advisor withdrew, and in what terms |

The last row matters more than it looks. An advisor who withdraws over a
disagreement and is then bound to silence about it has been managed rather than
consulted, and a studio that would want that clause should ask itself why.
