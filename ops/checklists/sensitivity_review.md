| **Owner** | `cultural-advisor` |

This gate runs **three times and is signed once**, at the third pass. Premise,
prompt set, cut. Running it once at the end is the failure the three-point structure
exists to prevent: review before generation, not before publication, because once a
striking image exists the conversation about whether it should exist becomes much
harder to win.

This is the one authority the Showrunner cannot unilaterally override.

## What this signature certifies

> *documentary-history:* The premise, the prompt set, and the locked cut clear
> cultural review. No restricted, sacred, or funerary material is depicted without a
> ruling. No unconsented likeness or voice appears.
>
> *narrative:* The treatment of the source tradition, and of the communities who hold
> it, clears review. No sacred or restricted material is depicted without a ruling.
> Depiction of real peoples and places is not defamatory or degrading.

## Pass 1 — premise, at `00_brief`

- [ ] The premise falls inside the line's advisory coverage, with named advisors competent on **these** traditions
- [ ] Categories requiring advisory review are identified in the brief rather than discovered later: sacred and ritual, masquerade and regalia, human remains and burial, living lineages, atrocity and enslavement, contested territory and title, bodies, language and naming
- [ ] Where the production touches a live dispute over land, chieftaincy, borders, succession, or origin, that fact is stated in the brief rather than pretended away
- [ ] Communities to be approached are named, with the recognised channel for each
- [ ] Consultation fees are budgeted — an unfunded consultation plan is not a plan
- [ ] *(narrative)* The interpretive stance is declared: which tradition, which variant, whose reading, and what the production is not claiming

## Pass 2 — prompt set, at `04_prompts`

**Before generation.** Not after.

- [ ] Every prompt card that touches a §2 category has been read individually
- [ ] No prompt would generate material whose viewing is restricted by initiation, gender, age, or office within the tradition concerned. **Wide availability online is not consent**
- [ ] No prompt generates human remains from a specific identifiable community without that community's agreement
- [ ] No prompt composites a real sacred object into a generated scene
- [ ] No prompt depicts a named living person
- [ ] No prompt generates identifiable victims of documented violence
- [ ] No prompt generates in the style of a living artist or a specific cultural custodian's work
- [ ] Prompts name the people, place, and period specifically. Generative models default hard to pan-regional pastiche and will produce it unless the prompt actively prevents it
- [ ] Sacred or ceremonial music is not being generated, in any form, for any purpose

## Pass 3 — the cut, at `08_review`

- [ ] The assembled cut has been watched end to end, with sound, at delivery quality
- [ ] No restricted, sacred, or funerary material appears without a written ruling recorded in the line's advisory register
- [ ] No unconsented likeness or voice appears — including of historical figures, for whom no consent is possible
- [ ] Every depiction is specific: the people, place, and period appear on the shot record and read correctly on screen
- [ ] Historical subjects have agency; they are not framed as objects of external forces
- [ ] Societies are shown with internal complexity — conflict, class, dissent, change — rather than as uniform
- [ ] Violence depicts consequence, not spectacle. The test: does the shot help the viewer understand what happened, or substitute affect for understanding?
- [ ] Warnings precede sequences involving atrocity, human remains, or graphic injury
- [ ] No overclaiming of grandeur to counter denigration. The corrective trap is still overclaiming and it hands critics an easy target
- [ ] Naming follows the entity records: self-designations primary, exonyms marked, contested names given both ways with the dispute named
- [ ] *(narrative)* Depiction of real peoples and places is not defamatory or degrading, and no "found footage" framing presents fabricated material as real
- [ ] Advisors who informed the material have been given the opportunity to review it **before** it airs
- [ ] Where a community asked that something not be shown, it is not shown — or the overriding public-interest reason is argued in writing, ruled on here, and disclosed on screen

## Holds

- [ ] Every open `[sensitivity]` hold on this production is resolved by a **written** ruling recorded in the advisory register
- [ ] No held item has been generated, edited, or advanced while under hold
- [ ] No hold was released by anyone other than this role
- [ ] Nobody who raised a hold has been penalised, excluded, or worked around

## Do not sign if

- **Any hold is open.** A hold is released by a written ruling, not by the passage of
  time or the arrival of a deadline.
- **You are signing pass 3 without having signed passes 1 and 2**, or without having
  seen the prompt set before generation. A single end-of-line review is a different
  gate with the same name, and it certifies nothing that could still have been changed
  cheaply.
- **The material is outside your declared competence.** Ruling anyway is worse than
  the delay. Escalate to an advisor on the line's register who is competent, and
  record that you did.
- **You are being told the sequence is too expensive to cut.** The cost of
  regeneration is borne by the studio; the cost of getting this wrong is borne by
  people outside it who had no route into the process.
- **An advisor's input was sought and then not incorporated**, without a written
  ruling saying why.
- **Wide online availability is being offered as evidence that material is not
  restricted.** It is not evidence of anything except circulation.
- **You signed another gate on this production.**
